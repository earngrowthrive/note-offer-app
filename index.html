Before You Calculate

What This Calculator Actually Does

This calculator helps you figure out what to bid on a performing note based on the yield you want to earn. You enter the note's current terms, the yield you are targeting, and the calculator tells you the maximum price to pay to hit that yield.

It is a starting point, not a final answer. A real offer needs stress testing against worst-case scenarios, ITV verification against the property value, and a full review of the collateral file. This tool gives you the math. The rest of the work is yours.

The UPB is the remaining balance the borrower owes. It is different from the original loan amount. Some payments have already been made, so the UPB is typically lower than what the loan started at. The seller of the note will list this number on the listing.

What the borrower pays every month, including principal and interest only. Do not include escrow for taxes and insurance.

$

Performing notes pay a fixed amount each month based on the loan's original terms. Enter only the principal and interest portion. If the seller's listing includes escrow, subtract that before entering this number.

How many months of payments are left on the loan.

mo

The remaining term is the number of monthly payments still owed by the borrower. A 30-year mortgage that has been paying for 5 years has 300 months remaining. Shorter remaining terms mean faster payoff but lower total interest collected.

The yield you want to earn on your investment, as a percentage. Common targets range from 8% (conservative) to 13%+ (higher risk).

%

Yield is what your money earns annually on this investment. A 12% yield means for every $1,000 you invest, you earn roughly $120 per year. Higher yields mean lower offer prices but also signal that the seller may be pricing in more risk. Stay anchored to what is realistic for the risk profile you want.

Acquisition & Servicing

What you'll spend to vet the deal before closing: title report, O&E, BPO, collateral file review.

$

Due diligence money is spent whether or not the deal closes, so it is part of your investment. The calculator subtracts it from the offer price so your target yield is on the total cash you put in, not just the purchase price.

What your loan servicer charges per month to collect payments and handle escrow.

$

Most note investors use a licensed servicer, and the fee comes out of every payment before you see a dime. The calculator prices the offer off your net monthly cash flow (payment minus servicing) so the yield you target is the yield you actually earn.

One More Check Before You Bid

The calculator gives you the price for your target yield. It does not check ITV (Investment-to-Value). Before you submit an offer, calculate ITV by dividing your offer price by the property's value. The rule from the buy box framework: stay at or below 75% ITV. Lower is better.

$

Stress Test This Number

Your offer price assumes the borrower keeps paying. Here is what to consider before submitting it:

  • What if three payments are missed in the next 12 months?
  • What if the property sells for 15% less than the current valuation?
  • What if foreclosure becomes necessary? Look up the timeline and legal costs in this property's state.
  • What if you have to hold longer than you planned? Does that still work for your portfolio?

If the math still works under each of these stress scenarios, the offer is solid. If not, lower your bid or pass on the deal.

What This Calculator Doesn't Do

This tool gives you the math for a single offer based on the inputs you provide. It does not evaluate the borrower's payment history. It does not assess the property's condition or location. It does not verify the chain of title or the assignment paperwork. It does not catch second liens, unpaid taxes, or code violations. Those skills are how you turn a calculator number into a real, defensible offer.
