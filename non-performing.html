Before You Calculate

What This Calculator Actually Does

A non-performing note has no payments coming in, so you cannot price it off a payment stream. You price it off the exit. This calculator models the path you have to be prepared for: foreclose, take the property, and sell it.

You enter the note's terms, the property's value and state, and the annual return you are targeting. The state sets the foreclosure process and timeline. The calculator then builds a month-by-month cash flow for that path (insurance, servicing, legal costs, then the property sale) and tells you the maximum price you can pay and still hit your target return. If the borrower starts paying again instead, that is a bonus. Never price a non-performing note as if the bonus is guaranteed.

Your Target

The annualized return you want if you have to take this deal all the way through foreclosure. Non-performing notes typically target 16 to 20 percent or more.

%

IRR (internal rate of return) accounts for both how much you make and how long your money is tied up. Non-performing notes demand higher target returns than performing notes because you're paying legal fees, insurance, and taxes for months or years before a single dollar comes back.

These defaults are round estimates from my own underwriting. Adjust them to your deal and your servicer. Legal costs for a routine foreclosure usually land between $2,000 and $5,000, and contested files run higher. Timelines vary by county, not just by state.

Stress Test This Number

Your offer price assumes the model's timeline and costs hold. Before you submit it, ask:

  • What if foreclosure takes twice as long in this county? Re-run the number with the longer timeline.
  • What if the BPO is optimistic and the property sells for 25% off instead of 15%?
  • What if there are unpaid property taxes, municipal liens, or code violations that come off the top?
  • What if the borrower files bankruptcy mid-foreclosure and adds 12 or more months?
  • If the state has a redemption period, can you carry the property until title is truly clear?

If the math still works under each of these, the offer is solid. If not, lower your bid or pass on the deal.

What This Calculator Doesn't Do

This tool prices one exit path with the numbers you give it. It does not verify the chain of title or the collateral file. It does not check your lien position, unpaid property taxes, or municipal liens, any of which can quietly eat your entire margin. It does not know whether the property is occupied or whether the borrower is a bankruptcy risk, and its state guidance is a starting point, not legal advice. Those skills are how you turn a calculator number into a real, defensible offer.
